One connected research programme

Aquaculture monitoring needs useful measurements without an expensive sensor for every parameter. This work explores estimating water quality from available measurements, learning the errors that physical equations leave behind, and predicting future conditions from time-series data.

This brief consolidates the research into three contributions. The linked papers and thesis provide the experimental detail; grant certificates document the patent records.

01 / Estimate the present

Virtual sensing for water-quality monitoring

Problem

Dedicated water-quality sensors increase the cost and maintenance burden of a monitoring system. Dissolved oxygen is a particularly important measurement for aquaculture.

Approach

Use relationships between available water measurements to estimate a target parameter instead of measuring every parameter with a separate sensor.

Contribution

This is the virtual-sensing foundation of the research programme. The thesis explains the monitoring context and the progression from conventional estimation to learned correction.

02 / Learn the residual error

Deep learning enhanced virtual sensing

Problem

Physical relationships provide a useful baseline, but their estimates can leave errors that vary with the measured conditions.

Approach

DLEVS combines dissolved-oxygen solubility and interdependency equations with recurrent models that learn the remaining error. The experiments compare LSTM, GRU, bidirectional models and attention mechanisms on ADAK and MAC data.

Reported results

The published study reports attention-based GRU RMSE reductions from 0.3802 to 0.1534 on ADAK and from 0.7505 to 0.0848 on MAC, compared with conventional virtual sensing.

These are results reported in the paper, not a fresh reproduction. The repository documents the experiment entry points and dataset requirements.

03 / Predict what comes next

Hybrid models for water-quality prediction

Problem

Monitoring describes current conditions. Prediction must account for how different water-quality parameters change over time.

Approach

Compare recurrent and hybrid convolutional-recurrent architectures. The implementation separates data preparation, model definitions, training and evaluation, with experiments on window size, batch size and learning rate.

Research outcome

The related prediction study was published in IEEE Access in 2022. Thesis comparisons show that no standalone model dominates every parameter and dataset, motivating hybrid approaches rather than a single universal winner.

The paper and thesis contain parameter-specific metrics and experimental settings. A related publication is not a substitute for the granted patent specification.
