Overview

My research connects environmental sensing and deep learning to study affordable water quality monitoring and accurate prediction for smart aquaculture. At NIT Puducherry, under Dr. Harigovindan V. P., I investigated how virtual sensors and sequence models could address measurement cost, prediction accuracy and computational requirements.

Thesis: Deep Learning Based Affordable Water Quality Monitoring and Accurate Prediction for Smart Aquaculture.

Focus

  • Monitoring systems: energy-aware measurement scheduling and solar energy harvesting.
  • Virtual sensing: estimating dissolved oxygen through solubility models and learned error correction.
  • Prediction: recurrent, hybrid and attention-based models for water quality time series.
  • Experimental workflows: model comparisons across parameters, configurations and prediction horizons.

Contributions

Connecting physical models with learned corrections

The virtual sensing work combines established dissolved oxygen solubility equations with an LSTM model that learns estimation error. The thesis evaluates this approach on ADAK and MAC data.

Comparing models before combining them

The research examines LSTM, GRU and bidirectional variants, then combines convolutional feature extraction with recurrent learning in CNN-LSTM and CNN-GRU models. The thesis reports that no standalone model dominates every parameter and dataset, supporting the study of hybrid approaches.

Extending sequence prediction with attention

Attention-based work studies how weighting sequence information can improve prediction. Related collaborative research explores stacked ensemble learning for dissolved oxygen.

Building reusable experiment workflows

The code archive includes data preparation, sequence windows, model comparisons, evaluation metrics and result exports for salinity, pH, dissolved oxygen and temperature.

Impact

The research contributes methods and comparative studies addressing three practical concerns: the cost of sensing, the accuracy of prediction and the computation needed to produce it. Its documented outcomes include journal articles, patent grants to NIT Puducherry and an integrated thesis connecting sensing systems with learning methods.

The demonstrated contribution is methodological. Farm-level savings, production gains and deployment scale have not been established in the audited outputs.
